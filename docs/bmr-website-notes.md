## The pitch, as the site tells it

BMR positions itself as bringing Formula One and MotoGP back to Africa, with Cape Town as the proposed home, and is explicit that this is "not a return to the past" but a purpose-built motorsport destination with a permanent economy that lives beyond race week. The circuit is described as the catalyst; the wider destination is the legacy. They call the project credible on three grounds: the FIA Grade 1 / FIM circuit, world-class infrastructure, and an experienced leadership team.

**Project status, their words:** BMR is advancing planning, design, approvals, funding and stakeholder engagement. The project "does not represent a secured Formula One or MotoGP race, calendar slot or agreement." They are engaging investors, partners and supporters.

## Circuit and race infrastructure

The vision page lists the circuit targets the map's layout checker already validates against:

- Approximately 4.8 km in length
- Start-finish straight of at least 600 m
- Full-throttle straight of at least 1 km
- Multiple overtaking possibilities
- Track width of at least 12 m throughout, incorporating natural elevation
- Grandstand and general-admission capacity of approximately 125,000 spectators per day
- A pit and paddock precinct that doubles as convention and exhibition space year-round

New detail for the map: the circuit is "developed from a layout with an established motorsport foundation, refined with specialist designers" and is "designed to work with the natural topography of the site." The site's imagery shows grandstands packed with crowds and a big race-day atmosphere.

## Site and location

The proposed site sits on the N7 corridor north of Cape Town, in an established regional growth corridor:

- Direct N7 frontage with multiple highway interchanges
- A railway station within walking distance of the circuit precinct (Kalbaskraal)
- Three airports within roughly 40 minutes, including an adjacent aerodrome
- Over 8,200 verified hotel rooms within a 40-minute radius
- A Table Mountain backdrop for broadcast and arrival

## The year-round destination

Six pillars, matching the map's precincts: Formula One & MotoGP; Conferences & Exhibitions; Entertainment & Amphitheatre; Hospitality & Waterfront; Residential & Lifestyle; Business & Innovation. The masterplan also lists research/technology, commercial & retail, and public realm & outdoor space.

## The team

- **Neal Froneman**, Executive Chairman: founding CEO of Sibanye-Stillwater, four decades leading large-scale international organisations.
- **Bobby Hartslief**, Chief Executive Officer: four decades in international motorsport, circuit development and major-event promotion, including Formula One Grands Prix and MotoGP World Championships; described as the driving force behind BMR.
- **Srinivasan Venkatakrishnan**, Director: former CEO of AngloGold Ashanti; governance and international capital markets.
- **Robert Appelbaum**, Director: senior South African corporate lawyer.

The organisation page stresses the year-round destination model and a leadership group spanning large-scale capital projects, international business, finance, law and motorsport promotion.

## Newsroom

One statement so far, dated 22 September 2026: BMR announced its intention to bring a Formula 1 Grand Prix to the Swartland region, positioning Cape Town, the Western Cape and South Africa as contenders to host Formula 1 in Africa by 2031. Announced by Froneman at the annual Swartland Mayoral Breakfast (hosted by Swartland mayor Harold Cleophas), attended by Acting Premier Tertius Simmers and provincial minister Anton Bredell. The project is described as entirely privately driven and funded.

## What this changes for the map

- The map's precinct set already mirrors the six destination pillars; the site confirms that framing is the one BMR leads with.
- The gallery imagery confirms the mood the map should hit: crowds, night lighting, water, trees, an arrival gateway. The current map's still, empty scenes are the gap.
- "Kalbaskraal fan walk" is BMR's own phrase; the map's fan walk should keep that name.
- Nothing on the site contradicts the demo brief's cautions: no masterplan published, no secured race, and the artist's-impression disclaimer is a reminder to keep invented specifics off the map.
